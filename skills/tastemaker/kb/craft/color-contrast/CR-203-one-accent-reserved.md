# One Accent, Reserved

## Rule
Pick **one** accent color and reserve it for the things that most need attention — primary actions, key interactive states, focus. A rough balance: ~60% neutral, ~30% secondary tone, ~10% accent. Don't let the accent appear on six different elements per screen.

## Why it matters
Color is a precision instrument for directing attention — but only if it's rationed. When one accent marks only the primary action, the eye finds that action instantly, every time, because nothing competes. When the accent is sprinkled across badges, links, icons, highlights, and the CTA, it can no longer lead anything — you've spent your loudest signal everywhere and have nothing left for the moment that matters. Restraint in color is what *creates* the accent's power. (This is the craft-floor expression of the taste principle TS-002, Unused Tools as Strength.)

## How to spot it
Count the distinct saturated/accent uses on one screen. The accent appearing 6+ times, or two-plus competing accents at similar saturation, dilutes the hierarchy. Linear's discipline — one lavender, used on the brand mark, focus, and primary CTA, and almost nowhere else — is the model.

## The fix
Demote everything that doesn't need the accent to neutral. Reserve the saturated accent for the single primary action and true interactive state. If two things both "need" the accent, decide which is actually primary.

## Eye-training
Squint at a screen and see where the color "pops." If it pops in five places, none of them leads. Train the instinct that every additional accent use is borrowed from the one that matters. Study restrained interfaces and notice how *little* color they use, and how powerful the little they use becomes.

## When to flex
Data viz, illustration, and playful/expressive consumer brands legitimately use a wider palette — there, color is content or personality, not just signal. The 60-30-10 split is a *heuristic* starting ratio, not law. The reserve-the-accent principle is strong for product UI where attention-direction is the job.
