# Density: Consistent and Context-Appropriate

## Rule
Information density should be (a) **consistent** within a surface and (b) **appropriate** to the context. A pro tool a power-user scans all day earns high density; a consumer onboarding screen earns low density. Within one surface, a region 3× denser than its neighbors — with no reason — is a defect. Pick a density and hold it.

## Why it matters
Density is right or wrong only relative to the job. Too sparse for a power tool wastes the expert's screen and forces scrolling; too dense for a consumer flow reads as stressful and intimidating. And *inconsistent* density within a screen — one cramped section next to airy ones — makes the layout feel unplanned, like different people designed different parts. (This is the craft-floor of the Taste KB's TS-007 "Earned Density": craft asks "is the density consistent and fitting?"; taste asks "does the density have rhythm and intention?")

## How to spot it
Scan for density jumps: a section noticeably tighter or looser than the rest with no contextual reason. Then sanity-check against context: is a high-stakes/consumer/first-run surface crammed, or a power-user data tool wastefully airy? Both are mismatches.

## The fix
Level inconsistent density — either give the cramped region room or justify its tightness (it's a data table; the rest isn't). Match overall density to the audience and stakes: loosen consumer/anxious surfaces, allow tightness in expert tools.

## Eye-training
Squint at the whole screen — do the regions feel like one consistent "weight," or does one block look notably heavier? And ask "who uses this, and would they want it tighter or airier?" Calibrate by comparing a Bloomberg-class tool (rightly dense) with a meditation-app onboarding (rightly airy) — both tasteful, opposite densities.

## When to flex
Deliberate density contrast is a tool (a dense data block intentionally set against airy framing to make it the focus). The rule guards against *accidental, unexplained* density jumps and against *context-mismatched* density — not against intentional, rhythmic variation (see CR-502).
