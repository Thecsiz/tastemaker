# Vertical Rhythm & Generous Defaults

## Rule
Default to **more** whitespace than feels necessary, then remove. Establish a consistent vertical rhythm down the page — predictable, scale-based gaps between sections — so the layout breathes and scans. Cramped is the more common failure than airy.

## Why it matters
Inexperienced layouts are almost always too tight — elements crowd because empty space feels "wasted." But generous, rhythmic spacing is the single clearest marker of a confident, expensive-feeling design. Whitespace isn't absence; it's a positive element that gives content focus and the eye places to rest. A page with consistent vertical rhythm feels composed; one with arbitrary section gaps feels assembled.

## How to spot it
Scroll and watch the section-to-section gaps. Are they consistent and generous, or arbitrary and tight? Content jammed against edges, sections with no breathing room between them, a page that feels "full" wall-to-wall — all tells of insufficient rhythm.

## The fix
Increase section spacing (from the scale — CR-001) until the page breathes, then pull back only if it feels disconnected. Make section gaps consistent. Give hero content and key moments extra room.

## Eye-training
Look at work you admire and notice how much *emptiness* it tolerates — far more than feels safe. Train the instinct that "too much space" is usually right and "just enough" is usually too tight. When a layout feels cramped, the fix is almost always more space, not smaller elements.

## When to flex
Density-appropriate contexts (a pro data tool, a dashboard a power-user scans all day) legitimately run tighter — see CR-501. Generosity is the default for consumer and content surfaces; it bends for genuine density needs, but even dense UIs need *rhythm*, just at a tighter pitch.
