# Editing over Completeness

## Ranking

**When a surface could show everything or show what matters, it shows what matters, even when showing everything would be denser, more complete, and defensible as "earned."**

## Why

TS-007 (Earned Density) and TS-010 (Editing vs. Listing) are both true, and they pull in opposite directions on the same screen. Density says a professional user can handle more and the layout can earn it with rhythm. Editing says someone has to decide what matters, and showing everything is a way of not deciding. Without a ranking, the critique splits the difference and the surface keeps growing.

This ranking sides with editing by default because completeness is the path of least resistance. Nobody is blamed for including a field. Every inclusion has an advocate and every cut has a cost someone can name. So surfaces drift toward completeness unless a ranking pushes back. The cost accepted here is that some users will occasionally need one more click, one more disclosure, or one more screen to reach the thing that was cut from the default view.

When building is cheap, this ranking matters more, not less. The constraint that used to force editing (it was expensive to build the extra panel) is gone, so the decision has to be made on purpose.

## How to Spot the Conflict

- A dense layout is defended as "earned" while several of its items are rarely used.
- A screen has grown one release at a time and nobody has re-edited the whole.
- The critique could cite both TS-007 and TS-010 for the same region, and they disagree.
- A feature list or scope review keeps every item because each one is individually justifiable.

Name the conflict, cite TR-002, and say which items should move behind disclosure, move elsewhere, or go.

## Exemplars

**Consumer-warm (opinionated task apps).** Task managers known for restraint ship fewer views and fewer fields than their competitors and say so openly. Users who need everything go elsewhere; users who stay get a tool that never asks them to sort through options they do not use.

**Editorial (the front page).** A front page is the purest editing exercise in design. Every story is newsworthy; a handful get placement. The value of the page is the ranking itself.

**Utilitarian (search home pages).** A search home page that is one input and almost nothing else could show news, weather, shortcuts and trending queries. Leaving them out is the product decision.

## When It Flips

- **Completeness is the job:** trading terminals, monitoring dashboards and reference tables exist so an expert can see everything at once. There TS-007 wins and TR-002 yields. Edit through grouping and rhythm, not removal.
- **Exhaustive surfaces:** a full settings catalog or admin panel must be complete; the editing shows up as search, filters and grouping that make completeness findable.
- **Regulated content:** disclosures that law requires on screen are not candidates for editing out.
